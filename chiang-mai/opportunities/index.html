1. Headline thesis — the rebirth of a builder hub in Chiang Mai

Chiang Mai has lost several of its builder anchors. OpenHub, a Nimman coworking space that ran weekly AI and developer hackathons in 2025, has gone quiet and its domain has lapsed (§2). The ʻohana itself first learned to build there. Punspace Nimman and Draper Startup House closed in the pandemic. MANA, The Hub (the container space) and Niramis appear to be gone. CAMP at Maya is no longer open 24 hours (Thai Holiday Guide 2026).

Demand from builders has not gone away. It has turned seasonal and event-driven:

When What Scale Source
10 Oct–10 Nov 2024 Edge City Lanna pop-up village 800 people (~330 full-month), ~1,000 events, 200 hackers, 90 projects; 24/7 workspace at Alt_PingRiver Edge City recap · CityLife
8 Dec 2025–3 Feb 2026 ETHChiangmai 2025 (bootcamp, unconference, hackathon, summit), co-hosted with Chiang Mai University "70+ unconference events, 1000+ participants, 44 hackathon projects, 120+ hackers" (organiser's own figures) ethchiangmai.com · X · Devfolio

The gap: pop-up cities arrive for a season and leave. We found no public evidence of a year-round, builder-first hub (event hall + coworking + café) in the Nimman–Huay Kaew–Santitham triangle that also serves the CMU student and startup community. The nearest are The Brick (CMU STeP), Yellow (now a Web3 incubator) and the Nimman venues that host the pop-up cities and ETHChiangmai side events (year-round public access: [verify]) (CMU STeP, Yellow).

Who could thrive in that kind of space now: - Web3 and AI nomads, and pop-up-city organisers who need a 6–10-week base every year. - Coworking and coliving brands that are expanding. - Specialty cafés, which need daytime traffic. - Music venues and listening bars. - Language, coding and art schools for the CMU student belt and international-school families. - Wellness and longevity studios. The Chiang Mai Chamber of Commerce is pushing a ฿150bn/year "longevity economy" target for 2031 (CityLife · Nation).

Why now (with the headwinds): - ✅ DTV visa (since 15 Jul 2024). Five years, 180 days per entry (+1 extension), ฿10,000 fee, ฿500,000 in funds. The "soft power" routes include Muay Thai, cooking, seminars and music festivals, which is the class/school/venue economy (dtv.in.th, PRD). More than 35,000 people had applied by July 2025 (Thaiger, citing IMI Daily). - ⚠️ Tighter DTV papers from 31 Aug 2026. Applicants now need proof of permanent residence where they apply, plus a criminal-record certificate (Consular Affairs notice, summarised at dtv.in.th). - ⚠️ Visa-exempt stays reportedly cut from 60 to 30 days for most passports from 15 Sep 2026, per an event organiser's FAQ (Xenoria City) [verify against the MFA]. - ⚠️ Tourism mix is shifting. Chinese visitors to Chiang Mai fell more than 30% in Apr–Jun 2025 (CityLife). Korean, Japanese and long-haul visitors are rising (Bangkok Post). The airport expects ~10M passengers in 2026 (ASEAN NOW). - ✅ Landlords are softer. Thai hotel closures rose 42% in Jan–Sep 2026, and some Chiang Mai hotels have closed for good (Thaiger, 28 Oct 2026). Dot Property lists 290 commercial rentals in the province (search), so tenants have room to negotiate. - ✅ Nightlife pilot. 2026 pilot late-hours (4am) zones in parts of Nimman and the Old City, for specially licensed venues (Out Thailand guide) [verify with the official notice].
