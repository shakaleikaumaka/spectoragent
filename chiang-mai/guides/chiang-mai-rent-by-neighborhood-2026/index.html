Chiang Mai rent by neighbourhood (2026)

Chiang Mai is still one of the cheapest comfortable cities in Asia to rent in, but prices in the popular areas have risen and the gap between neighbourhoods is large. A practical rule of thumb from local rental guides: the same room can cost about twice as much in Nimman as in the Old City or Santitham (Monthly Chiang Mai).

This guide gives 2026 ranges for a long-term (6–12 month) lease, area by area, and then the things that change your real monthly cost: utilities, deposits, season and air quality.

Area by area

Nimman — convenience at a premium

The default landing zone for remote workers: the densest cluster of cafés and coworking spaces, Maya and One Nimman malls, modern condos with pools. You can live without a scooter. The trade-off is the highest rent in the city, traffic and noise at peak times (BAANLYY). Some 2026 guides report 1-beds in central areas rising from roughly ฿8,000–12,000 a few years ago to ฿12,000–18,000 now (Superagent).

Good for: first months in the city, social nomads, people who hate commuting. Watch for: electricity mark-ups in serviced buildings; short-let "condos" that are really unlicensed daily rentals.

Old City — character and walkability

Inside the moat, height limits keep buildings low, so stock is smaller apartments, low-rise condos and converted houses. Very walkable, Sunday Walking Street on your doorstep, more tourists and some noisy streets at night.

Good for: culture lovers, people who want to walk everywhere, mid-length stays.

Santitham — the value pick next to Nimman

Between Nimman and the Old City, dense and very Thai, with fresh markets and cheap food. Older buildings, fewer luxury towers, but often the best rent for the location. One 2026 cost-of-living guide quotes clean 1-beds at ฿6,000–9,000 here (Thai-AEC).

Good for: long-stayers on a budget who still want Nimman within a short walk or ride.

Chang Phueak and Suthep/CMU

North of the moat (Chang Phueak) is local and quieter; the Suthep/Chiang Mai University side is greener, near Doi Suthep, popular with students and staff. Prices sit between Santitham and Nimman.

Riverside / Night Bazaar (Chang Klan, Wat Ket)

East of the Old City toward the Ping River: hotel-style serviced apartments, riverside restaurants, close to the airport and markets. Condo choice is thinner than Nimman; the Night Bazaar strip is touristy.

Hang Dong, San Sai, Mae Rim — space, gardens, a car

Families and retirees often head to the suburbs for a house with a garden or pool at a fraction of Bangkok prices. You will need a car or scooter for everything. Hang Dong is close to international schools such as Panyaden; Mae Rim is home to Prem Tinsulanonda International School; San Sai is a calmer, cheaper residential spread to the north-east (BAANLYY). Confirm school details directly with each school.

What changes your real monthly cost

1. Electricity (the number people underestimate)

The grid rate is roughly ฿4–5 per unit, but some condos and landlords charge ฿7–8, and serviced apartments can charge ฿10+ per unit. Light use in the cool season might be ฿800–1,500 a month; heavy air-con in the hot season can reach ฿3,000–5,000 (Monthly Chiang Mai). Ask for the per-unit rate in writing before you sign.

If your landlord is a business operator (under the 2025 Contract Committee notification, landlords offering three or more residential units), utilities must be charged at the official supplier rate, with no mark-up (ThailandLawOnline's translation of the 2025 notification — verify the current text with a lawyer). Private landlords with one or two units are generally outside that rule.

Water is usually ฿100–300 a month; fibre internet ฿500–800 (Monthly Chiang Mai).

2. Deposits and move-in money

A common setup is 1–2 months' deposit plus the first month in advance — so a ฿12,000 room may need ฿24,000–36,000 on day one (Monthly Chiang Mai). For covered business landlords, the 2025 notification caps deposit + advance rent (generally three months' rent for monthly-paid leases) and sets deposit-return deadlines (ThailandLawOnline). See our renting checklist for details.

3. Lease length

6–12 months gets the best rate. Month-to-month and serviced apartments typically cost 20–40% more, especially from November to February (Monthly Chiang Mai).

4. Season

  • Cool season (Nov–Feb): best weather, highest demand, least negotiating room. Book early for specific buildings.
  • Hot season (Mar–May): electricity bills jump.
  • Rainy season (Jun–Oct): green city, most negotiating power for tenants.

5. Burning season and air quality (Feb–Apr)

Crop and forest burning can push PM2.5 to unhealthy levels, with bad AQI days in the 150–300 range in some years (Monthly Chiang Mai). Many residents leave, run air purifiers or wear N95 masks. If you have asthma or young children, ask about sealed windows and purifier-friendly units, and consider timing your lease so you can travel in March–April.

How to use these numbers

  1. Pick the area by daily life first (walkability, school run, coworking), then compare buildings by total monthly cost = rent + electricity at that building's per-unit rate + internet + transport.
  2. Compare 3–5 similar listings in the same building or street — that's your real comp, not a citywide average.
  3. Don't over-commit on day one. A month or two in a serviced room or a short lease lets you test the area before signing 12 months.
  4. Where to look: established Thai-registered local agencies (Thailand has no specific agent licence, so check registration and track record), large portals (e.g. FazWaz, Hipflat, DDproperty), and building juristic offices. Be careful with deals that appear only in social-media groups (see the scams section of our renting checklist).
